# Design UI

Run only when explicitly invoked by the human. Do not activate other skills automatically. Follow the [workflow](../../../docs/workflow.md) and relevant project guide; design work does not authorize production implementation or bypass test and validation gates.

## Establish the brief

Inspect the target product, existing interface, components, tokens, content and applicable guidance. State In Scope and Out of Scope around one complete task or representative screen. Separate confirmed requirements from assumptions. For a redesign, identify what works and should survive before proposing changes.

Resolve audience, primary task, desired character and available material from evidence. Ask only about missing decisions that materially affect the proposal. Inventory real product screenshots, images, claims and copy; mark placeholders. Never fabricate testimonials, customers, metrics or credentials.

## Choose a direction

Use a small number of relevant references when helpful. Explain what each contributes and what should not be copied. References inform decisions; they do not replace understanding the product.

When direction is uncertain, compare two or three alternatives on the same content and task. Vary composition, hierarchy or interaction meaningfully rather than recoloring a template. When the direction is already accepted, develop it instead of reopening the choice.

Connect typography, density, spacing, color, imagery, voice and motion to the product's use and character. Identify a recognizable trait that can recur coherently, such as a distinctive timeline or treatment of product imagery. Familiar controls are appropriate when they support efficient work. Do not force novelty, asymmetry or ornament.

Treat fonts, gradients, cards and layout patterns as contextual choices, not universal bans. Distinguish marketing pages from working application screens: a dashboard does not need a hero, editorial whitespace or a display font to have identity.

## Make the proposal assessable

Prefer an inspectable visual proposal when tools and scope permit: a mockup or isolated prototype using representative content. Label its fidelity, placeholder content and unimplemented behavior. A prototype is not accepted production code. If only prose is possible, state that visual quality remains unverified.

Show the primary task, narrow-width adaptation and relevant loading, empty, error and success states at a fidelity appropriate to the decision. Consider keyboard use, focus, zoom, long content and reduced motion. Do not invent states the interaction cannot reach.

Present the direction, reasons, tradeoffs, what is preserved and concrete manual validation steps. Let the navigator choose or validate the direction before treating it as accepted project guidance. Record an accepted direction only in an authorized project location, preferably a short note linked from the project guide. Keep actual values in existing tokens/components rather than duplicating them in prose.

Implementation follows the project's normal story and pairing workflow. Suggest a next skill only when useful; wait for explicit invocation.
